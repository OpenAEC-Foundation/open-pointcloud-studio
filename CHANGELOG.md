## Unreleased

- **Extensions** from other authors can be installed. An extension is a folder, or a `.zip` of one, with an `extension.json` and a program in any language, such as a PowerShell or Python script, that drives the window through the local API in a process of its own, so that an extension that fails never takes the window down. **Install extension…** on the Extensions page of the File view checks it strictly (its manifest, at most 1000 files and 64 MiB, no links, nothing outside its folder, no archive entry that reaches outside, icons that refer to nothing outside themselves) and then shows what it declares, its author, version, command and what it uses, and asks to confirm before it is installed in `extensions/<id>` of the settings folder. The page lists the built-in and the installed extensions with their switch, version, author, **Run** or **Stop**, **Show folder** and **Uninstall…**; a newer version installs over the one installed and keeps its logs and its switch. Installed extensions are kept in `extensions.json`, which is now also read with a byte-order mark; two windows with the same settings keep each other's installs and switches.
- The buttons of the installed extensions are in the new EXTENSIONS group of the ribbon, after SELECTION, where the default window shows them, and their tiles on the New and Export pages of the File view. A button starts its extension, with `OPS_API_PORT`, `OPS_API_TOKEN`, `OPS_EXTENSION_ID` and `OPS_CONTEXT`, a file with the active scan, the selection, the section box, the view shown and the language of the window; while it runs the button is highlighted, a click on it stops it, and the status bar shows its name, its progress and **Stop**. When it ends, the status bar shows its message, or the exit code and the last lines of its errors. Each run writes a log in the folder of the extension, and the token of a run accepts only the commands the extension declared. Stopping a run, and closing the window during it, ends the programs it started; a run that ends by itself leaves open what it opened, such as a report in its program.
- The local API and the MCP server have `install_extension` (which shows the confirmation in the window), `run_extension`, `stop_extension`, `show_message`, `report_progress`, `choose_path` (a dialog of the window that asks the user for a file or a folder, as a job) and `context`; `list_extensions` lists the installed extensions with what they declare, `set_extension_enabled` switches them, and `status` reports the runs as `extensions`. The answers of the API name their charset, UTF-8. [Writing an extension](docs/extensions.md) describes the format for authors, with an example in PowerShell and Python that counts the points of the open scans.
- **Distance** measures between two points and starts a new distance with the next click, instead of going on from the last point.
- The Project Browser reads calmer: a row of VIEWS shows its icon and its name on one line, a long name ends in an ellipsis with the whole name in its tooltip, and the buttons of a row (rename, update, duplicate, ×) are on the row of what the window shows only. **Create 2D plan / elevation / section…** and **Open drawing…** are icons on the band of VIEWS, and BCF has one **Export BCF…** button with what the file would hold in its tooltip. A new elevation of the whole model is named without "· 3D model".
- The buttons of the ribbon sit closer together.
- **Annotations of drawings**: the VIEWS group of the ribbon has **Text**, **Dimension** and **Leader** beside Note and Line, each enabled where it applies and explained in its tooltip. On a plan, an elevation or a section, a text takes a click and its text, a dimension two points that snap to the ends and corners of the lines and a click for its line, a leader its arrow point and the place of its text, a line two points. A dimension shows the distance in the model in millimetres, rounded to 10 mm at 1:100 and 5 mm at 1:50, unless a value is typed. Annotations are kept with the drawing at their points in the model, so they stay when it is made again; their sizes are those of the paper at the annotation scale of the drawing, 2.5 mm text at 1:100 by default. Properties lists them with ×; a selected one can be dragged and its text or value changed. On a sheet a drawing shows its annotations at the scale of its viewport, **Text** and **Line** draw on the paper, and the PDF holds them. **Export DXF/DWG…** writes a drawing with its annotations: texts, real DIMENSION entities with their definition points on the layer OPS-DIMENSIONS, leaders and lines; reading such a file draws the dimensions from their points. The local API and the MCP server have `annotate_drawing`, `annotate_sheet` and `export_drawing_file`, `delete_annotation` also takes the `id` of an annotation of a drawing or a sheet, and `status` reports `drawing_annotations`.
- **Locks**: **Locked** in Properties locks the saved 3D view or the drawing that is shown, or a view placed on a sheet; the shown row of a drawing has a padlock among its buttons too, and the row of what is locked has a padlock for its icon. While a locked 3D view is shown, or lies behind a drawing or a sheet, orbiting, panning, zooming, walking, the view cube and the section box leave it as it is and the status bar says "*name* is locked"; **Update** is dimmed, and renaming, duplicating (the copy is unlocked), deleting and annotating still work. A locked drawing keeps its crop region, its turn with RO and the points it uses, and still pans and zooms. A locked view on a sheet is not moved, resized, scaled or removed. The lock is kept, and the tab of a locked view or drawing shows a padlock that unlocks it. The local API and the MCP server have `lock_view` and `unlock_view`, and the status, `list_drawings`, `list_sheets` and `list_tabs` report the locks.
- **Sheets**: a new group SHEETS in the Project Browser, between VIEWS and BCF, holds sheets of paper from A4 to A0, lying or standing, with a border and a title block (project, name, number, scale or "as indicated", date and who drew it), kept beside the saved views. **New sheet…** asks for the number, the name and the paper, A3 lying by default; every row has Rename, Duplicate and ×. A sheet opens in a tab: the paper on grey, with pan and zoom. Drag a row of VIEWS onto the paper, or use **Place view** in Properties: a plan, an elevation or a section is placed as vectors at 1:100 or another scale (1:20, 1:50, 1:200, 1:500 or typed), showing its crop region with every layer; a saved 3D view as its snapshot at about 200 dpi, in the proportions of its picture. Each view has its title under it, is selected with a click, moved with a drag and taken off with Delete, and follows its view: a drawing made again, a view renamed or updated show on the sheet, and a deleted view leaves "view missing". Drawings on a sheet that are not made yet are made in the background while it is shown. **Export PDF…** writes the sheet as a PDF the size of its paper, with the drawings and the title block as vectors. The local API and the MCP server have `list_sheets`, `create_sheet`, `update_sheet`, `duplicate_sheet`, `delete_sheet`, `show_sheet`, `place_view`, `update_viewport`, `remove_viewport` and `export_sheet_pdf`.
- The snapshot of a saved view, the picture a BCF file holds and a sheet shows, leaves out the view cube, the handles of the section box, the selection and measurements: they step aside for the moment it is taken.
- The name field with **Save view** is at the top of VIEWS in the Project Browser, above the list of views.
- The 3D model and the views and drawings lie side by side as tabs above the scene. **3D model** comes first and never closes; every saved view, plan, elevation, section and file opened from VIEWS opens a tab, or activates its tab when it is open. A click on a tab shows it, its × or a middle click closes the tab without deleting anything, and Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab step through the tabs. The active tab is marked in the accent colour and its row under VIEWS stays highlighted. The 3D model keeps its own camera, section box and colour mode while a saved view is shown, and a drawing keeps its zoom. Long names shrink with an ellipsis and a tooltip, copies in the middle so that their number stays, and the strip scrolls when the tabs still do not fit. The open tabs and the active one are kept for the next session. The tabs replace the line that said MODEL SPACE and ISOMETRIC; the strip says it at its right. The local API and the MCP server have `list_tabs`, `show_tab` and `close_tab`, and `status` reports `view_tabs`.
- **Points used (%)** in **Create 2D plan / elevation / section…** makes a drawing from a share of the points of the scans, 10% by default: the same points whatever the crop region, spread over every scan. The filled cut of a plan is still traced from every point, so a plan keeps all its walls with fewer points drawn. The **Crop region** section of Properties has the same field, and the local API and the MCP server take `sample_percent` in `create_drawing` and `set_sheet_crop`.
- A drawing keeps the points it read: after a change of its crop region it is made again from them without reading the scans, and a region that grows reads only what it adds; the status bar says so. On a scan of over a hundred million points an elevation is made again in about 0.2 s instead of 3.5 to 6 s, and made in 0.7 s instead of 5.4 s; the parts of the index are read on several threads. Another cut or view depth, or another share of the points of an elevation or a section, reads again. The points are kept exactly as they were read, so the drawing is the one a read makes. Each drawing keeps at most 384 MB, all together 1 GB, the drawing used longest ago letting go first, and closing a scan lets go of the points kept from it.
- A click on the outline of the crop region selects it: it is drawn thicker with its handles, and its figures, with the points used, come first in Properties. Escape or a click elsewhere on the drawing deselects it; unselected it is a thin line without handles. The local API and the MCP server have `select_crop_region`, and `status` reports `crop_selected` and the points the drawings keep.
- Switching the section box on or off, dragging one of its faces or turning it no longer builds the points on screen again and sends them to the graphics card: the box only clips what is drawn, in the next frame, and the points stay as they are.
- The section box no longer makes the points load again: a moment after it stops changing, only the part of the scan inside it is read from the index, in more detail, and the points this adds are drawn beside those shown, up to the budget for all that the box shows and without a frame that shows fewer points than the one before. Nothing is read when the box shows about a whole budget or all the points there already, or was read for the same view and the same points before. Switched off, the box shows the points of the view at once and puts the detail read inside it aside until it is switched on again; when the view moved while the box was on, that detail stays in sight until the view has been read for the new camera. While the box is on, the points of the view outside it stay on the graphics card without being drawn, so up to about twice the budget is held. **View sample** in Properties counts the points drawn, with the box on those inside it, and `status` in the local API reports the points read inside the box per scan as `focus_sample`. **Refresh LOD** reads the view again also when nothing changed.
- A click on the outline of the **crop region** in the Drawing view selects it: it is drawn heavier, Properties names it under its title and starts with its **Crop region** section, where its width, height, centre, rotation, cut and view depth are typed and applied with Enter, while it stays selected. A handle let go selects it too; Escape or a click beside it lets go of it. The local API and the MCP server have `select_crop`, and `status` reports `drawing_view.crop_selected`.
- **Mesh Pointcloud** is the one button of the SURFACE group, in the place of Closed mesh, Terrain mesh, 3D surface and Detect faces. It opens a card in three steps. **Method** offers the four ways as cards, each saying what it makes and when to use it (**Closed mesh**, **Terrain mesh**, **3D surface** and **Flat faces**), and below them what they work on: the active scan, the section box with about how many points lie in it, and the selection, with buttons that switch the box and fit it to the selection. **Options** has the settings of that method with their defaults beside them, an explanation under the pointer and **Use recommended**, says what a job would do and why it cannot start yet. **Run** shows the progress with Cancel and, at the end, the figures with **Show in model**, **Export…** and **Back to options**. Each method keeps its own options.
- A mesh job goes on when the card of Mesh Pointcloud is closed: the button in the ribbon is then highlighted and says which step of the job runs and how far that step is, such as **Step 2/4 · 77%**, and opens the card again on its Run step.
- On the Run step of Mesh Pointcloud, **Show in model** and **Export…** act on the scan that holds the result shown, also when another scan is active, and wait with a line that says why when no scan holds it any more. The terrain mesh and the 3D surface each keep their own result, and the button at the bottom reads **Run** until the method has a result and **Run again** after.
- The settings of the closed mesh, of the faces and of the 3D surface left Properties for the options of Mesh Pointcloud. Properties keeps what the active scan holds: under **Surface mesh** its mesh, with the deviation and the advice of a closed mesh, and under **Detected faces** its faces with their list, colouring and exports.
- The local API and the MCP server have `mesh_wizard`, which shows the card of Mesh Pointcloud on a step and with a method or takes it away, and `status` reports the card with what the methods work on. The commands that start the jobs and set their settings are as before.
- With the section box on, the photo marks, their path and the rings to step into show only the photos taken inside the box, as the points do.
- The **Select** button left the ribbon: without a tool the mouse selects, and Escape returns to it.
- A click on the mark of a photo enters it; a double click is no longer needed. Inside a photo, or while walking, the nearest photos ahead show as rings, and a click on a ring steps into that photo.
- **Distance** and **Area** measure inside a photo and while walking: a click without a drag picks the point under the pointer.
- A plan made from the whole 3D model in **Create 2D plan / elevation / section…** is cut 1.20 m above the floor that the points show, also when the scan lies far above or below zero, as a surveyed scan does; before, the dialog proposed 1.20 m above zero and refused it for such a scan. The dialog says at which height it found the floor.
- A large button of the ribbon is as wide as its name, so that a long name such as **Snedetekening** is no longer broken in the middle of the word. **Move**, **Scale** and **Thin** under Transform in Properties are icons, named in their tooltip, so that the narrow panel no longer breaks their names.
- The Linux packages on the release page carry a build attestation kept by GitHub: with the GitHub CLI, `gh attestation verify` checks that the project's release workflow attested the file and that it has not been changed since. The release notes and the README give the full command.
- Where the section box cuts a mesh, the cut looks solid: the material between the two faces of a wall, floor or ceiling is filled on the faces of the box in one colour, as in a section drawing, instead of showing the hollow between them. Only two faces that lie opposite each other at most the maximum wall thickness apart are filled, never a single surface such as a facade scanned from one side, a loose sheet or the open edge of a mesh. In a closed mesh made without stations, whose faces all look at the middle of the region, the walls are found from the turns of air and material along the cut. **Fill the cut**, **Max. wall thickness (m)** (0.50 by default) and **Cap colour** under **Section box** in Properties set it, and are kept with the display settings; the local API and the MCP server have `set_section_fill`, and `status` reports `section_fill`. A screenshot and the snapshot of a saved view wait until the cut is filled.
- A mesh of large triangles, such as a model of a few walls, is drawn with the right face in front: inside a large triangle the depth was off, so that a face behind another could show through it, and the section box clipped it a little off its faces. A triangle that reaches behind the camera, as when walking through a mesh or zooming in close, is now cut off at the eye instead of disappearing whole.
- `--survey INPUT OUTPUT.json` prepares a scanned building for Pointcloud to Drawing without a window: it finds the box around the building from the index without the stray points far below or far out, and the main direction of the walls, reads the scene once along that direction into cells of 5 by 5 cm and 2 cm high on all threads, takes out groups of fewer than 20 cells as noise, and proposes the footprint from the walls, with a second direction when a wing stands at another angle. It finds the levels in the horizontal area per height within the footprint: the floors with their ceilings, a suspended ceiling under its slab, the thickness of the slabs, the roof and the ground, with desks and counters left out as furniture. Each floor is then read again to the millimetre, with its slope in millimetres per metre, and the plan of a storey is cut 1.20 m above its floor, or up to 0.15 m higher or lower where a counter lies at that height. P, the height that is zero, is the lowest whole floor above the ground. It counts the stray points more than 2 m below the lowest floor, or below the box where the site slopes down further, one by one, with the clusters of at least 25 points among them. It writes the box, the directions, the footprint, the cells per height, the levels and the counts as JSON and prints a line per level. It gives the same result for any number of threads and keeps to 512 MB, with wider cells for a large scene.
- **Pointcloud to Drawing**, a large button in the new POINTCLOUD TO DRAWING group of the ribbon and a tile on the New and Export pages of the File view, opens the wizard that is to make plans, sections, elevations, a site plan and a model of a building from its scan. This version has its frame: a card over the window with the steps from preparation to the result in a sidebar, a dot per step that tells where it stands, a column for the settings of a step, a preview, and the buttons Close, Previous, Run this step, Next and Run all automatically. Next is dimmed until a step is confirmed or skipped and says why it waits. Step 0 finds the levels of the building; the later steps do not compute anything yet.
- **Show in model** makes the card of the Pointcloud to Drawing wizard a strip above the scene with Previous, Next and **Back to wizard**, so that the model can be looked at and the section box moved while the wizard waits. The strip is never part of a screenshot, a view snapshot or a BCF image; while the card is shown the keys of the model do nothing and a screenshot through the local API is refused. Escape closes Settings first, then makes the card the strip, and closes the File view after that.
- The local API and the MCP server have `mesh_to_plans_action`, which runs, confirms, skips or cancels a step of the wizard, goes back or on, or opens a saved project, and `mesh_to_plans_level`, which selects, renames, moves, cuts, shows, adds, merges or removes a level of step 0 or makes it P; `status` reports the project and the frame, footprint and levels that step 0 found.
- The local API and the MCP server have `mesh_to_plans_view`, which shows the wizard as card or strip on a step or takes it away, and `status` reports the wizard with the state of every step.
- **Run this step** and **Run all automatically** in the Pointcloud to Drawing wizard run steps in the background, one job at a time, with a line in the progress strip and Cancel; a step that ended waits for **Confirm**, and Run all automatically confirms each step itself. The steps that ended keep their result when a job is cancelled, and Exit cancels a running job. The steps after step 0 still compute nothing: each stands in for its work for about a second. The local API reports the job in `status` and as a job that `job` and `wait_for_job` read, and `wait_until_idle` waits for it.
- Step 0 of the Pointcloud to Drawing wizard, **Preparation**, finds the building in the shown scans: the box around it without the stray points far out, the main direction of its walls, the footprint and the levels, each floor to the millimetre with its slope, and counts the stray points more than 2 m below the lowest floor one by one, with the clusters among them. The preview shows a histogram of the horizontal area per height with a line per floor that can be dragged in steps of 5 cm, or to the millimetre with Shift; a view from above coloured by height with the boxes of the site, the core and the building, the footprint and the main directions; and a table of the levels with floor and ceiling above P, storey height, slab, cut, slope, confidence and whether a level was found or edited. Levels can be renamed, added, merged and removed, with the slabs measured again from the ceilings to the floors above, any whole floor can be made P, and the cut of a plan, 1.20 m above its floor by default, can be changed per level. **Show in model** puts the section box on a storey and frames it in the model, also when the Drawing view was shown, while the wizard waits as a strip, and puts the box back afterwards; the survey can read the section box instead of the whole scan, and **Section box to building** puts the box around the building. **Confirm levels** locks them, **Edit levels** opens them again.
- A Pointcloud to Drawing project is kept in a folder of its own, by default `Documents\OPS Pointcloud to Drawing\<name>`, or `<name> 2` and so on when a project is there already; a project is never written over another, and the page offers to resume the one that is there: `project.ops-m2p.json` with the scans, the frame, the boxes, the state of every step and the levels, written whole a moment after every change and at once when the window closes or another project is resumed, and `survey\profile.csv` and `survey\top.png` beside it. The Project Browser offers **Resume Pointcloud to Drawing (step n)** for a recent project whose scans are open, and opens it on the first step that is not done; when a scan changed since, step 0 is marked out of date. It is out of date at once when a scan moves, loses points or has a class hidden, or the core or the main direction changes while the wizard is open, and as it was when the change is taken back; run again, it keeps the levels that were edited by hand.
- The Project Browser has four groups under bands of their own colour, each with an icon, its name and a count: **SCANS**, **CLASSES**, **VIEWS** and **BCF**. A click on a band collapses the group or opens it again, and the window remembers it; collapsing never closes or hides anything. Every row has the small icon of its kind, so a scan never looks like a drawing.
- The band of SCANS shows how many scans are open and their points, a switch that shows or hides all scans (a dash while only some are shown), and buttons to add a point cloud and to open a scan folder. While scans open or index, a line with a thin bar says how many are ready, also when the group is collapsed. Scans from more than one folder are grouped per folder, each folder with its own band, switch and count.
- **VIEWS** takes the place of DRAWINGS and VIEWS AND SECTIONS and holds everything that is a view: the 3D model at the top, then the saved views, the plans, the elevations and the sections, and the previews, exports and opened DXF and DWG files, each kind under its own band that collapses. A click shows the view or the drawing; the row of what is shown is highlighted. **3D model** lets go of the active view, so that the 3D model is the row highlighted.
- One **Save view** button: a view keeps the section box, with its limits and its turn, while the box is on. Restoring a view switches its box on, or the box off for a view without one. A view is of the 3D scene: with a drawing or the File view in front, Save view and Update show the scene and save it, so the snapshot of the view is never a picture of a drawing. **Rename**, **Update**, **Duplicate** and × are small buttons on every row of a view, and none of them restores the view first. A view with a box has a small box mark in the list, and the BCF export writes its box as the clipping planes of its viewpoint. **Save section** is gone: section boxes saved with it become views the first time, one per box, framing it.
- **Create 2D plan / elevation / section…** makes its drawings from the whole model, the section box or a saved view with a section box. Every drawing it makes keeps how it was made and is listed under VIEWS after a restart; a click makes it again when its scans are open, or says which scans to open. These drawings are never pushed off the list by newer previews or files.
- The local API and the MCP server have `create_drawing`, `list_drawings`, `show_drawing` (which shows the 3D model by the name `3D model`), `delete_drawing` and `set_browser_group`, and `status` reports the groups of the Project Browser.
- A plan, elevation or section of the Project Browser shows its **crop region** in the Drawing view: a thin rectangle with a handle in the middle of each side and at each corner. Dragging a handle moves that side, with the size shown in metres and in whole centimetres; on release the drawing is made again under the same name, at the same zoom and place. When you choose the 3D model, a view, another drawing or the File view while it is made, the window stays there. The **Crop region** switch with the layers hides it, and it is never written into a DXF or DWG file.
- The **Crop region** section of Properties sets the width, height, centre, cut and view depth of the drawing shown, and the rotation of a plan; Enter applies a value.
- Every row under VIEWS but the files has a **Duplicate** button beside its ×: the copy is named "… (2)", listed right below the original and shown, and changes on its own. A drawing is copied with what was drawn, without computing it again; one not drawn yet in this session is copied only when it can be drawn at once, so a refused copy leaves nothing behind. Duplicating **3D model** saves the current 3D view as a view.
- Typing **R** and then **O** turns the crop region of a plan in the Drawing view with the pointer (Shift for steps of 15°, or type the angle); Enter or a click applies it, and the plan is made again upright with the model turned, so that walls at an angle run along the sheet. In the 3D view RO turns the section box the same way; while walking, type the angle. Any other key between R and O, also Space, Enter, Tab, an arrow or Escape, starts over, and a section box set otherwise while it turns, by restoring a view or typing its limits, ends the turn and stays as set.
- The local API and the MCP server have `set_sheet_crop`, `drag_crop_handle`, `duplicate_view` and `rotate_crop`, `list_drawings` gives the crop region of each drawing, and `status` reports a turn under way and, as `project_browser.shown`, the row of VIEWS that is highlighted. `save_camera_view` and `update_camera_view` show the 3D scene when the Drawing view was shown. `screenshot` with `window` captures the whole window as the application draws it, also when the window is not on a screen.
- A scan that is being opened shows its points on the way instead of only its station marker until it is done: at every tenth of its points the scene gets an even sample of all that has been read so far, so a scan project fills in from the first seconds. Ten scans of ten million points opened together showed their first points after a quarter of a second instead of after two to three seconds, and were all open in the same time as before. The scan that is opened in the end is the same as before.
- When many scans are read side by side, their new points are shown together, a few times a second, and the camera frames the scene once, when the first points appear; after that it stays where it is. This holds for E57, PLY and PCD files and for a LAS or LAZ file that has to be read in full; a large PTX or text file gets its points every few seconds. The detail of the scans that have an index already is read for the camera meanwhile and stays: the points of the other scans coming in do not interrupt it.
- Indexes are built several at a time: one for every four cores of the computer, at most four, and fewer when little memory is free. Ten scans of ten million points were indexed in about 20 s instead of about 45 s one after the other. The other scans wait their turn, the active scan first, and one file is never indexed twice at once.
- Every row of the Project Browser shows how far its own index is, and the strip above the scene says how many indexes are ready, how many are built at once and how many wait.
- **Cancel index** stops every index that is being built or waits. A scan that was being indexed while it was opened stays open, without its index: its reading goes on as a line of the strip of its own, whose **Cancel** still stops it. The scans that were open are not indexed automatically again until **Auto-index** is switched off and on. **Build index** still builds the index of one of them, and when every place is taken it puts the active scan in the queue ahead of the scans that are indexed automatically.
- The progress strip above the scene is readable in the light theme: its text was white on the white scene.
- The local API and the MCP server report the running and waiting index builds in `status.result.index`; `build_index` answers `queued: true` when the scan waits for a place, and `cancel_index` says how many builds it stopped and how many scans it took out of the queue.
- A LAS or LAZ file whose points cannot be read, such as a cut-off LAZ file, is closed again with the reason in the status bar, instead of staying as a layer without points that waited for its index for ever; one with an index kept from before stays open from that index, without the points shown while it was read.
- A LAZ file whose compressed chunks vary in size is read in full for its first picture, with its points shown at every tenth on the way, instead of being sampled at places in it that cannot be found from the number of a point.
- E57 files with panoramas or photos taken along a path show their photos. Equirectangular and cylindrical panoramas, and pinhole photos of a camera that measured no points, are read with their own position and direction; a pinhole photo whose pixel size is zero or empty gives its focal length in pixels, as some programs write them, where such photos were left out before. The photos are marked in the scene along their path while **Stations** is on, and each scan lists them in the Project Browser under a line such as "12 photos".
- Double-click a photo mark, or a photo in the Project Browser, to stand where it was taken with the photo laid over the points: a panorama is looked around like a station panorama, a pinhole photo is seen from its own camera with its field of view. The **Photo** slider sets how much the photo covers the points, `Page Up` and `Page Down` or the arrows beside the slider go along the path, and `Esc` returns the camera to where it was, also into the station panorama a photo was entered from. Clicking, measuring and annotations work on the points under the photo. Photos are decoded in the background at most 4096 pixels wide, with the next and the previous one read ahead, a few at a time while `Page Down` is held; a photo that cannot be decoded says so under the scene.
- Properties shows how many panoramas and pinhole photos a scan has, and the coordinate system its file states, such as an EPSG code.

## 0.4.0 - 2026-10-02

- A new native desktop application replaces the earlier one, which is archived. It needs no web view and comes as one executable for Windows, Linux and macOS.
- Opens point clouds in LAS, LAZ, E57, PLY, PCD, PTX, PTS and text formats, and meshes in OBJ, OFF, STL and DXF.
- Surveys of many gigabytes stay usable: a preview appears first, detail for the current view is read from an index on disk, and a scan that was opened before reopens from a cache.
- A whole scan project opens at once from its folder, from its scan project file (`.rcp`), or by dropping either on the window.
- The 3D view has a view cube, camera presets, colouring by stored colour, elevation, intensity or classification, eye-dome lighting and a point budget of up to ten million points.
- Scanner stations of E57, PCD and PTX scans are shown in the scene. Stations with photos are drawn as balls: click one to stand in that station and look around, and walk through the scene with W, A, S and D.
- Points can be selected exactly by box or by picking, deleted with undo and redo, thinned, moved and scaled; a section box limits what is shown and exported.
- Exports the whole cloud, the selection, everything but the selection, the section box or every Nth point as LAS, LAZ, E57, PLY, XYZ, PTS or CSV, and merges several LAS or LAZ scans into one file.
- Builds a terrain mesh or a 3D surface from the points and saves it as OBJ.
- Camera views can be saved per scan and restored.
- Five colour themes, a File view for opening, exporting and merging, and a ribbon with all tools.
- A local command API drives a running window from scripts, and command-line modes export, cut a section, index, merge and mesh without opening a window.
- Each release archive carries the licence texts and has a checksum file beside it.
